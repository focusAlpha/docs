Japan IR Presentation Decks are the slides a Japanese company posts on its own investor-relations website: results-briefing decks (決算説明会資料), medium-term-plan and investor-day presentations, broker-conference decks, and other investor materials. Each row is one entry on the site, with links to its documents and — on request — their extracted text. This is what the company **showed**; what was said aloud is in [Japan IR Transcripts](/international/japan-ir-transcripts).

<Info>
  **Plan:** Free and above · **Credits:** 1 per call
</Info>

## What you can use Japan IR presentation decks for

Read the charts and KPIs management chose to show at a results briefing. Follow a medium-term plan from the day it was presented. See how a company pitches itself at broker conferences between results. Compare a strategy-day deck with the forecasts in [Management Guidance](/events/guidance).

## Deck subtypes

| `subtype` | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `results_briefing` | Results-briefing decks, investor and information meetings |
| `strategy_briefing` | Medium-term plans, investor and IR days, ESG and business briefings |
| `call_or_webcast` | A results call or webcast entry that has a document posted with it |
| `investor_conference` | Broker conferences and dialogues with institutional investors; trade shows and product seminars are deliberately excluded |
| `other_material` | Other decks, integrated reports, fact books |

**Not here**, and in [Japan IR Website Disclosures](/international/japan-ir-web-disclosures) instead: the earnings release (決算短信, `results_summary` — a results statement, not a deck), a webcast entry with nothing posted behind it (`webcast_link`), and forecast-revision notices.

What a row is was decided from its **title**, not from the section of the website it was listed under. `site_type` keeps the section the site used.

## Japan IR website coverage

Coverage is a fixed set of about 250 Japanese companies whose investor-relations websites are read continuously — not the whole market. `coverage.companies_covered` gives the current count; `coverage.available_from` and `available_to` give the date range for the company you asked about. History reaches the late 1990s where a site keeps an archive, and only recent years for most.

<Warning>
`company.in_ir_web_universe: false` means FocusAlpha does not read that company's investor-relations website. It never means the company posted no decks.
</Warning>

The endpoint covers **Japanese companies only**: any other company returns `404`; for US companies see [US Presentation Decks](/transcripts/us-presentation-decks). If the website source cannot be read, the endpoint returns `503` rather than an empty list.

## Key Japan IR presentation deck fields

- `title` and `title_language` (`en` or `ja`) — as the site wrote it, untranslated.
- `kind` is `presentation_deck`; `subtype` is one of the values above.
- `page_url` is the page the entry was found on; `media_urls` are the linked files.
- `documents` — each linked document's `format` (`pdf` or `html`), `url` and `chars`, longest first. `has_text` says whether any text was extracted; about 30% of website rows have none, which means "not extracted", not "empty".

The response also carries `company` (`company_id`, `ir_site_tickers`, `in_ir_web_universe`) and `counts`: `matched`, `in_this_tool`, `by_subtype`, `undated_excluded_by_date_filter` and `truncated_at_source`.

## Japan IR website date convention

Only a real calendar day becomes `date`; `date_status` is `date`, `datetime`, `period_label` (a fiscal period such as `2003-H1`, never turned into a day), `ambiguous` (such as `01/03/19`, not guessed), `invalid` or `missing`, and `date_as_written` keeps the original. Rows come newest first — dated rows, then period-labelled rows, then undated rows. `date_gte` and `date_lte` apply to `date` only, and rows without one are counted in `counts.undated_excluded_by_date_filter`. See [Japan IR Transcripts](/international/japan-ir-transcripts#japan-ir-website-date-convention) for the full table.

## Japan IR presentation deck text

Pass `include_text=true` to add each document's extracted `text`, capped at `max_chars` per document, with `text_truncated`. Text is extracted from PDFs, so slides read as fragments and tables lose their layout: quote it, but take reported figures from [Japan Financials](/international/japan-financials).

## GET /v1/companies/\{company\}/jp/ir-presentation-decks

<ParamField path="company" type="string" required>
  Tokyo symbol (`6758.T` or `6758`), five-digit EDINET security code, EDINET filer code, ADR symbol (`SONY`), ISIN or `company_id` of a Japanese company.
</ParamField>

<ParamField query="subtype" type="string">
  `results_briefing`, `strategy_briefing`, `call_or_webcast`, `investor_conference` or `other_material`. Any other value returns `400`.
</ParamField>

<ParamField query="q" type="string">
  Case-insensitive substring of the title, English or Japanese.
</ParamField>

<ParamField query="date_gte" type="string">
  Earliest `date`, `YYYY-MM-DD`; `date_lte` sets the ceiling. Undated rows are excluded and counted.
</ParamField>

<ParamField query="include_text" type="string" default="false">
  `true` adds each document's extracted text.
</ParamField>

<ParamField query="max_chars" type="integer" default="20000">
  Text cap per document when `include_text=true`, between 1,000 and 200,000.
</ParamField>

<ParamField query="limit" type="integer" default="50">
  Rows per page, between 1 and 200. Defaults to 10 when `include_text=true`. Page through with `cursor`, passing back `next_cursor` unchanged.
</ParamField>
